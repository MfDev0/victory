VIctory Try 6.19 "no light in blocks" (the first 6.18 did not build on your PC: 6.19 has all of 6.18, below, and goes after the square lights)
- The build error of the first 6.18 (a name defined twice, TARGETS_AT) is fixed: VIctory Try builds again, with everything in 6.18. Every change in 6.19 was compiled and type-checked before it was pushed (the same check finds the 6.18 error)
- Light in blocks (1): the game packs light into textures in its own memory with compute shaders (as R32_UINT) and reads it back through another view of the same memory (as R9G9B9E5): light probes, bounced light. When the writing view was one the game placed in memory itself, VIctory gave it a null view ("placed unordered texture view not mirrored" in your log): the light it wrote went nowhere, and what read it saw memory nothing had written. Those views now get a texture of their own on the card, and the reading views find it (from cool_fox e11479ae, #72: NetherySiloX, Gregory Bonzon, ryotdev; VICTORY_PLACED_UAV=0 goes back)
- Light in blocks (2): on NVIDIA cards the water's two tiled light shaders can only be rebuilt in one form, and that form leaves whole tiles of open water dark (around boats, along shores, under bridges) and costs about 15 ms of the card a frame. They are left out there: the water is a little darker and bluer, with no blocks, and faster (from trustno1-coder/VIctory-NVIDIA 35c15608; VICTORY_WATER_LIGHT=1 runs them)
- Memory: while the card stands past its budget, new textures leave out a third level as well (a sixteenth of a big texture's memory instead of a quarter); they get it back when there is room (VICTORY_TEXTURE_OVER_DROP=0 goes back)
- Log: every shader rebuilt in an approximate form is named by hash (in prepare.log and the game log) and counted in the frame report (VICTORY_WAVE_WIDE_FIRST=1 tries the other form first for them, off by default); new lines give the render targets that start at the same address in the game's memory (memory the console shares between them and VIctory does not yet), the textures made for the placed views above, and the indirect work not run, by why

VIctory Try 6.18 "nothing let go that comes back" (the white and grey people, the first seconds, and a log that says more; from the 6.17 report)
- Picture: why people and objects flickered black, white or grey and came in late (6.17 report, RTX 4050 Laptop 6 GB, the card at 5,628 MB of its 5,153 MB budget): to stay under the card's line, VIctory lets go of the card's copy of a texture nothing has used for a second. On this card that was about 10,000 textures a minute (700-750 MB): a character who turned away, or a table behind the camera, lost its textures a second later, and when it came back into view each one was shown as a placeholder (grey for colour, white for masks: the white arms and faces) until it was uploaded again, behind 2,000 others waiting for memory. Now a texture that came back once is kept for 30 s unused before it may go again (VICTORY_SWEEP_BACK_MS), and every texture needs 8 unused frames, not 4 (at 3 fps, 4 frames were a second and a half)
- Picture and memory: the card's copies of the game's render targets (screens, shadows, depth) were never let go: they grew from 385 MB to 1,383 MB in five minutes and pushed the card past its budget, so Windows moved memory out to the PC and everything else had to be let go instead. Now render-target copies that nothing holds go when the game has given their memory up, or (while the card is past its budget) when nothing has used them for two minutes (VICTORY_TARGET_SWEEP=1: only the first; 0: as before)
- The first seconds: after a loading screen the world was held back while its textures uploaded (up to 10 s; 2.8 s at a frame a second in your report, which looked like seconds of nothing). Now 1.5 s at most (VICTORY_JOIN_HOLD_MS sets it; 0: off)
- Log: a new line says how many textures the sweep let go and how many came back (each a placeholder until its upload), how many it kept because they came back, how many render-target copies went and why, and the placeholders by kind (grey, flat normal, white)
- Found, not changed: the five "refused" compute pipelines in the log are ray-tracing passes left out on purpose (B, rt-off list), and the five shaders that do not translate ("a descriptor read only as data") are only made at the menu and never used in the world: neither is the square lights

VIctory Try 6.17 "no wasted processor" (two causes on the processor side found in the 6.15 report)
- Speed: the game waits for its fences with two console instructions (monitorx, then mwaitx) that a PC cannot run, so each one takes an exception into Windows and back. Since 6.8 VIctory rewrites them in the game's code the first time, so they take no exception again. That worked for mwaitx, but the hot monitorx sits across two 8-byte words, where the rewrite did not fit: in the 6.15 report it failed 66 million times (patched 1, unpatched 65,974,989) and took an exception 440,000 times a second, more than a whole processor of the laptop's power budget. It is now made a 3-byte NOP that fits (it keeps its first byte and changes the other two in one store). A place that cannot be patched is no longer tried again at every exception, and the first reason is written in the log
- Speed: the pass over the game's descriptor heap (the 25,000-30,000 records the game copies into its view heap itself every frame: 22-39 ms) runs on a thread of its own beside the preparing of the submission, instead of before it on the thread the card waits for (the card stood idle 68-112 ms a frame behind it). Off again by itself if the card is lost; config.json beside=off
- Smoother frames: the pass that readies the game's buffer views for its shaders looked again at all 150,000 of them every 16th time, which made those frames take 100-180 ms of it instead of about 40. It now looks at a sixteenth of them every time, the same looks spread evenly (Kelvaroq 0055's idea; VICTORY_HEAP_AUDIT_SPREAD=0 goes back)
- The fork's copy: VIctory Try also looks for the fork at MfDev0/CATez (a repository of its own outside the GitHub fork network) when MfDev0/victory cannot be reached

VIctory Try 6.16 "room for windows" (the 3 fps cause in the 6.15 report, the flickering faces, and the disk)
- Why it was still 3 fps (6.15 report, RTX 4050 Laptop 6 GB, MAX on, 300-420 ms a frame): textures filled the card to 5,050-5,100 MB of its 5,153 MB for the whole world (MAX let them take 99%). The game's memory windows, which most draws read, then found no room on the card: 117 went in through Resizable BAR, and the 768 after them stayed in the PC's memory, which the card reads across the laptop's bus at every read (0 on the card). The card was busy about 200 ms of every frame. In the NVIDIA fork's measurements, windows on the card took a 3060 from 1.6-3 to 10-12 fps
- Speed: room on the card is now kept for windows (up to a quarter of the card, less what they already hold): textures stop before it, and windows may go to the card up to 96% of it (80 before). MAX on cards under 8 GB lets textures take 94% (99 before) and starts the memory guard at 95/98%. The frame line names where the windows' memory is (BAR, card, PC). VICTORY_WINDOWS_ROOM=0 goes back; VICTORY_WINDOWS_ROOM_MB sets the room
- Speed and faces: the texture rush (the scene first without the largest texture levels) went on and off 209 times in six minutes. Each time it ended, thousands of soft textures were made due again for their detail, which started the rush again, which made them soft again: 111,497 uploads, 52 GB in six minutes, 35-51 ms of every frame. And each change put a face's texture back to the placeholder for a moment: white (the placeholder of the face's detail maps), blue-grey or see-through. Now a texture made due for its detail is uploaded whole, and the rush rests 30 s after it ends (VICTORY_TEXTURE_RUSH_GAP_S)
- The disk: each version translated all 25,746 of the game's shaders again (about 1 GB) and kept every older version's beside them. Now VIctory keeps the two most recently used builds' translations and removes the rest 90 s into a run (VICTORY_SHADER_CACHE_KEEP); the 6.15-and-older cache goes once. VIctory Try also removes the engines not in use (victory-avm, victory-8gb and their build and cache folders; N builds them again), empties the build folder past 12 GB, and keeps the newest 10 game logs and 2 fork backups. TIDY shows what takes what. config.json tidy=off: nothing is removed
- Every report now has the card's time by kind of list and by the game's passes (VICTORY_CARD_METER=1, two timestamps a list), also without the frame log

VIctory Try 6.15 "new things fast" (objects, shaders and places that come into view are drawn at once; fixes from cool_fox and Kelvaroq)
- New objects drawn at once: when something new comes into view whose shader is still compiling, VIctory now draws it with a finished shader of the same kind (same shaders, only culling, depth test or blend differ) in that very frame, before any wait, and swaps in the real one as soon as it is ready (Kelvaroq 0050's idea). Before, on your PC these draws were simply dropped (stand-ins had been off since an old lost card): 19-24 missing draws a frame when the world streamed. The frame then also stopped up to half a second for the compile; that wait is now 250 ms at most, and only for things with no stand-in
- No more stalls when shaders are saved: VIctory saved its whole compiled-shader file (1.1 GB) every 20-40 s while new things came into view, and every shader finished during a save waited for it. In the 6.12 report those frames took 0.4 to 5.2 seconds. Now a finished shader is used at once and stored after the save (cool_fox 4a028f31, Esteban Viera), the save runs on a thread of its own instead of a shader compiler thread, and in the world it comes at most every 5 minutes (behind loading screens every 20 s, at the end always)
- Stability: a shader whose translation crashes no longer takes a compiler thread down with it and leaves that object missing for the rest of the run (cool_fox 744c09ba, Filip Michalski)
- Stability: a texture seen through a second view is held alive while it is copied: the game could free it in between, a crash or garbage on screen (cool_fox 94f35b7f, cccw)
- The start screen names stand-ins ON or off; a lost card turns them off for the next run (after wide hashes and one frame ahead)

VIctory Try 6.14 "one frame ahead" (the cause of 3-4 fps found and fixed; ideas from Kelvaroq and the Xbox Series S)
- Why it was 3-4 fps (6.12 report, RTX 4060, MAX on, 220 ms a frame): 130 ms of every frame the copy-back thread waited for the card to fetch what its shaders wrote, because that fetch stood in the same queue behind every frame the card still had to draw. The fast way (the card's separate copy engine) had been turned off for good on this PC by a lost card long ago, together with barrier trimming, so the card also drew with every full stop between passes. And the game heard a frame was done only after the whole copy-back. MAX lifts limits, but none of these was a limit, so it changed nothing; the 6.13 Resizable BAR fix moves memory windows, and this PC had 0 MB of them, so it could not help here either
- Speed: what the card's shaders wrote is fetched on the card's copy engine again, beside the drawing, not behind it (expected: the 130 ms wait a frame falls to a few ms). Barrier trimming is on again: the card no longer stops all work for barriers that guard nothing. Each now has its own switch, and they are the last things a lost card turns off
- Speed: one frame ahead, as on the console. On a Series S the game records the next frame while the GPU draws this one, and its present returns at once. Now a thread of VIctory's own prepares and sends the game's work, and the present goes in line behind it (VICTORY_ASYNC_SUBMIT=1, VICTORY_ASYNC_PRESENT=1, Kelvaroq's defaults: worst frames 568 -> 262 ms there). The game is never more than one frame ahead
- Speed: lazy copy-back is on again for one more try: the game hears that a frame is done before the results are copied back, and waits only if it actually reads them (it had gone off after an old crash; a new crash on a memory access turns it off again by itself)
- Speed: the card checks which parts of the game's memory changed with 64 threads per 64 KB piece instead of one thread walking 4,096 steps (VICTORY_CARD_HASH_WIDE=1; fork #1014 by martindevbr, Kelvaroq 0041: heavy frames 198 -> 120 ms with it on an RTX 5080). Neighbouring pieces are fetched in one copy (Kelvaroq 0032)
- The start screen names each of these as ON or off. If the card is lost, they go off one at a time on the next runs: wide hashes first, then one frame ahead, then the rest, with copy-engine fetches last

VIctory Try 6.13 "NVIDIA fixes" (from a deep search of the NVIDIA forks: trustno1-coder/VIctory-NVIDIA and edsuuu/VI-BR-PORT, ported into this fork's engine)
- Memory: on PCs with less than 24 GB of memory, the game's memory windows go straight into the card's memory through Resizable BAR: one copy, none in the PC's memory, nothing to send each frame (Indy2l at trustno1-coder/VIctory-NVIDIA: on a 16 GB RTX 3060 PC the out-of-memory error 0x8007000E and the driver crash a minute in went away, shared memory stayed at 4.4 GB instead of 7.5, 1.6-3 -> 10-12 fps). If the card is lost with it on, it goes off by itself for the next run. VICTORY_WINDOWS_BAR_MB sets it by hand
- Speed: texture read-backs (the water maps the game reads back) are written into the game's memory by a thread of their own. Before, every finished frame waited for them, 100-400 ms in frames where the world streamed (VICTORY_TEXBACK_ASYNC=0 goes back)
- Shader stutter: a pipeline that is still compiling is waited for in one frame only; the frames after it go on (the object appears once it is ready) instead of freezing half a second a frame. Compute shaders start compiling as soon as the game creates them
- Picture: the face and skin decoration bake is found by name (51 programs, not 4 hashes): cutscene faces no longer come out black or flat
- Picture: streamed textures are no longer forgotten when the game changes a lot of memory at once (the list of changed memory is merged when full, never emptied)
- Stability: the card's buffer compare goes to the card on its own submission (D3D12 error 538 on NVIDIA)

VIctory Try 6.12 "all-out" (no caps mode; ray-only passes left out; fixes from edsuuu/VI-BR-PORT and trustno1-coder/VIctory-NVIDIA ported into this fork's engine)
- MAX (type MAX, then Enter): all-out mode. VIctory Try reads this PC (CPU threads, memory, card memory) and lifts VIctory's own caps to all of it but about 1%: every CPU thread but one for copy-back, texture detiling and pipeline compiles; the process at high priority (never realtime) with a soft hold on 90% of the memory; textures up to 99% of the card (90 before), no texture cap (the low preset's 1536 MB), 512 MB of texture uploads a frame (192), more of the game's buffers compared on the card, 256 MB DirectStorage reads in flight; the memory guard holds uploads back only at about 1% free (1 GB before) and the card at 98-99% full (85-97). If the card is lost, all-out goes off by itself for the next run. VICTORY_TRY_ALL_OUT=1 turns it on too
- Speed: with ray tracing skipped (B), the game's passes that only serve rays are not compiled and not run: the 30 bvh_skinning programs that pose every character a second time just for the ray structures (the largest share of the card's time in a cutscene: 6.8 -> 10.2 fps on an RTX 4060 Ti 8 GB at edsuuu/VI-BR-PORT), about 1,000 CS_RaytracingResolve programs, the probe ray picking, and the traces against structures that are never built (the Voxels and Fallback forms still run and light the picture). Also about 1,000 fewer pipelines to compile before and during play
- Picture: those ray-resolve passes, and 34 traced-shadow ones, turned every missed ray into light: rooms and reflections went white and hazy with the view angle (trustno1-coder/VIctory-NVIDIA's list, left out with B)
- Picture: a colour a shader writes that is NaN or infinite becomes 0 (VICTORY_NAN_SCRUB, trustno1-coder/VIctory-NVIDIA): one bad value no longer spreads through the blur and the upscaler into black blobs on faces and blocky shine where light reflects
- Every pipeline compile setting can be told: VICTORY_PIPELINE_WORKERS, VICTORY_PIPELINE_HEAVY; the memory guard's limits: VICTORY_PRESSURE_RAM_MB, VICTORY_PRESSURE_COMMIT_MB, VICTORY_PRESSURE_CARD_PERCENT (each "mild,critical"); VICTORY_PRIORITY=high; VICTORY_WORKING_SET_PERCENT

VIctory Try 6.11 "no disk stutter" (fixes from lepotane, AvatarManz and the others ported into this fork's engine)
- Shader stutter: the compiled pipelines (the 773 MB pipeline library) are read into memory before play on 16 GB PCs too. In the 6.7 report this was skipped ("too little memory free": it wanted 4 GB spare on top), so every pipeline the game needed in play was read from the disk page by page (seconds each). Now 1 GB spare is enough, and with less the front of the file is read
- Once per build, with the game closed, every shader in the game's files is translated into the cache (as AvatarManz's "victory shaders" does): a pipeline met for the first time in play then only needs the driver's compile. The first time takes 10-30 minutes; stopping it is fine. VICTORY_TRY_ALL_SHADERS=0 skips it
- Speed: parts of the game's submissions that follow one another go to the card as one (VICTORY_JOIN_PARTS, in the Y set; off by itself if the card is lost), and the copy-back work is cut into 256 KB pieces instead of 1 MB so all 12 threads share it (lepotane/VIctory-leonida)
- Picture: anisotropic samplers with point mips stay so on cards that have it (AvatarManz 011735ff; the RTX 4050 does), instead of blending between texture sizes the console does not
- The "check" test of the repository passes again (Rust sources may be up to 1 MB)
